Abstract

Automatic DJ systems mostly target electronic dance music, whose regular structure makes rule-based mixing relatively easier. Vocal-rich popular music is harder: which track to play next, where to cue it, and which transition to use are tied together, so fixed rules handle them poorly. We present a pop AI-DJ that puts an LLM in a reason-propose-verify-repair loop and lets it choose the next track, the cue points, and the mixing technique together. The LLM samples several transition plans; an external acoustic checker then scores the rendered audio and triggers a repair when a check fails. Because it samples, the system can keep several candidate transitions for one track pair instead of one fixed mix. In blind listening tests our transitions beat Mosaikbox, a reimplemented crossfade, and a cue baseline that reuses the crossfade's cues; that last comparison shows most of that margin comes from cue placement. The same planner also picks and orders a set along an emotion arc: this improves story coherence over a no-story ablation, and within a set story and transition ratings show no measurable gap from a human DJ. The checker mainly adds reliability, not higher average quality: it repairs rare but audible errors such as cut vocals, while the planner stays non-deterministic.
